# Global Happiness & Well-Being Analysis: Insight Report
This analysis examines happiness scores across countries alongside key socioeconomic factors that contribute to national well being using `World Happiness Report` data from `2017 to 2019`. The goal of this report is to guide resource allocation and development strategies for NGOs working to improve quality of life globally.

## Key Findings
Across all three years, the happiest countries are consistently those with strong `GDP`, `high healthy life expectancy`, and `stable social support systems`. This suggests that countries with strong economies are better able to provide the kind of support systems that contribute to national happiness. `GDP per capita (0.80)`, `Social support (0.76)`, and `Healthy life expectancy (0.75)` are the factors most strongly associated with happiness Score. `Generosity`, on the other hand, shows almost no correlation with `Score (0.12)` and `near 0 correlation` with most other factors. Notably, `GDP`, `Social support`, and `Life expectancy` are moderately correlated with each other `(0.64–0.78)`, suggesting these top factors overlap rather than acting as fully independent drivers of happiness.

## Supporting Patterns
`Western countries`, particularly in `Europe` and the `Americas` lead the world in average happiness largely due to their highly developed and stable economies. `Asian` countries show a more moderate average happiness score reflecting the region's diverse mix of cultures, economies, and circumstances. `African` countries on the other hand report the lowest average happiness scores, largely tied to challenging economic conditions, poverty, and concerns around safety and rights.

## Important Caveat
While factors like `wealth` and `health` strongly align with higher national happiness, these relationships represent correlations, not proof of cause and effect. Higher income does not automatically guarantee happiness, nor does lower income guarantee unhappiness rather, `wealth` often creates the conditions under which `social trust`, `healthcare access`, and `personal freedom` can thrive.

## Takeaway for Policy & NGO Action
Rather than focusing solely on `GDP growth`, NGOs and international policy organizations should prioritize `strengthening community support systems` and `healthcare access` beacuse these are the factors most closely tied to happiness outcomes, independent of a country's wealth alone.
